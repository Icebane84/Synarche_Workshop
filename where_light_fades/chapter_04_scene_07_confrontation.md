# Chapter 4, Scene 7: The Empath's Fear

**ID:** `narr-act-02-ch-04-sc-07`
**Version:** v1.0
**Scene Type:** Core Narrative Prose

---

Garrett’s departure left a silence in the chamber that was heavier and more complex than before. Kaelen stood, his back to the door, caught in the crossfire of his own thoughts. Garrett’s logic was a cold, sharp blade, and Valerius’s words were a comforting poison. He didn’t know which to trust.

The door opened again. He didn't have to turn to know it was her. Serafina’s presence had its own unique weight, a gentle warmth that felt like a sunbeam on his chilled skin. This time, however, it was accompanied by a quiet, steely resolve.

“In the library,” she began, her voice soft but stripped of its usual tentative gentleness. “You looked at me with such anger. Why?”

Kaelen turned, his own defenses flaring instantly. The whispers, which had been momentarily silenced by Garrett’s intrusion, returned with venomous glee. *She pities you. The broken little Paladin. Show her your strength.*

“I don’t need your pity, Serafina,” he snapped, the words colder and crueler than he intended.

She didn’t flinch. She didn’t recoil. She simply took a step closer, her green eyes holding his, her expression one of profound, aching sorrow.

“Do you truly believe that’s what you saw?” she asked, her voice trembling slightly, not with fear, but with the effort of holding back her own pain. “Pity? I looked at you... and I didn't recognize you.”

A beat of silence hung between them, heavy and fragile.

“That terrified me,” she whispered, the word a confession that shattered his anger. She took a shaky breath. "Not because I thought you would hurt me. Because I thought I was losing you."

Her hand came up, not to touch him, but to rest over her own heart.

"I am watching you build your own prison..."

Silence.

"And I don't know how to reach you anymore."

The raw, unvarnished honesty of her words disarmed him completely. He had no defense against it. He couldn’t tell her the truth—that he pushed her away because he feared the darkness in him was a contagion that would consume her light. He couldn’t explain that every moment of her gentle concern felt like he was leading her to a slaughter.

Her fingers lifted an inch, reaching toward him almost without thought. She stopped herself, letting her hand fall back to her side. The space between them suddenly felt impossibly wide.

So he said nothing. He simply stood there, defeated, the chilling logic of his brother and the comforting poison of his mentor now joined by the heartbreaking terror of the woman who loved him. He was surrounded on all sides, not by enemies, but by the very people he was trying to protect. And the walls of his prison grew higher still.
