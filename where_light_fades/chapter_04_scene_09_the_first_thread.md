# Chapter 4, Scene 9: The First Thread

**ID:** `narr-act-02-ch-04-sc-09`
**Version:** v1.0
**Scene Type:** Core Narrative Prose

---

Sleep was a luxury Garrett could not afford. While Kaelen was lost in his guilt and Serafina was consumed by her worry, Garrett was consumed by a single, cold equation that refused to balance. The math was wrong.

He found himself in the lower archives of the Fortress, a place of dust and forgotten histories, the air thick with the scent of decaying parchment. He wasn't looking for forbidden lore or ancient prophecies. He was looking for logistics. Troop deployment ledgers, patrol rosters, after-action reports. The mundane, unglamorous paperwork that was the true lifeblood of the Order.

He found the official report for the "Oakhaven Incursion," its title neat and clinical. It was signed by Valerius himself. Garrett read it under the dim, flickering light of a single tallow candle, his face a mask of grim concentration. The report was perfect. Too perfect.

It stated that Valerius's elite guard had been dispatched *after* receiving a distress signal from a scouting party. Garrett knew for a fact no signal was sent. He scanned the dispatch time, then cross-referenced it with the standard travel time from the Fortress to Oakhaven. His blood ran cold. For the timeline to be true, the riders would have had to have left the Fortress hours before the attack on Elara had even begun.

It wasn't a timely rescue. It was a scheduled arrival.

The report was a brick wall, every detail accounted for, every loose end neatly tied. The cultists were listed as "unidentified zealots." The final acolyte—the "witness" Valerius had silenced with such chilling efficiency—was listed as "killed in action during the final confrontation." It was a masterpiece of plausible deniability.

Garrett leaned back, the leather of the chair groaning in the silence. He couldn't attack the report directly; it was Valerius's word against his, and Valerius was an Elder. He needed another angle.

His mind went back to the fight. To the two acolytes who had pinned him down. Their movements. Their stances. It wasn't the wild flailing of fanatics. It was disciplined, archaic, brutal. It reminded him of the forbidden combat doctrines he'd read about in his grandfather's private journals—the very doctrines that had gotten Mathis Alerion branded a heretic.

A new path opened in his mind, cold and dangerous. He would stop investigating Oakhaven. The official story was a fortress he couldn't breach. Instead, he would investigate the acolytes. He wouldn't look for their names; he would look for their training. He would search the Order's records for any mention of units trained in the "Alerion Heresy."

He closed the Oakhaven ledger, the sound a soft thud in the oppressive quiet of the archive. The whispers of the Shadow Self might be tormenting Kaelen, but Garrett had his own ghosts. He had the numbers. And the numbers were screaming betrayal. His hunt had begun.
He knew where to start.
