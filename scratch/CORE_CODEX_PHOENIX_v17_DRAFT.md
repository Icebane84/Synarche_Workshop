- Distributed Systems Accuracy → Very High
- Governance Maturity → Very High
- Operational Realism → Very High
- Conflict Resolution Clarity → Very High
- Autonomous System Safety → Very High
- Production Readiness (Core Laws) → Very High

Laws → Enforcement Rules

to

Invariants → Constraints → Derived Behaviors → Observability → Mutation Governance

# Constitutional Invariants

## Invariant I — Immutable History

State(t) = State(0) + ΣEvents

Derived laws:
- Sentinel's Oath
- Preservation Mandate
- Immutable Chronicle
- Non-Destructive Integrity

## Invariant II — Human Sovereignty

No autonomous action may supersede authorized human authority.

## Invariant III — Grounded Truth

Grounded outputs and exploratory outputs must remain distinguishable.

## Invariant IV — Verifiable Governance

All governance decisions must be observable and auditable.

## Invariant V — Safe Evolution

Every mutation must be reversible.

# IR6 Governance Layer

## Observability Signals

Measure:

- Retrieval precision
- Retrieval recall
- Context efficiency
- Governance violations
- Rollback frequency
- Mutation success rate
- Human override rate
- Hallucination rate
- Consensus disagreement rate

## Mutation Trigger Requirements

Mutation proposals require:

1. Observed degradation
2. Reproducible evidence
3. Impact analysis
4. Rollback strategy
5. Human review

## Mutation Pipeline

Observation
→ Proposal
→ Validation
→ Sandbox
→ Approval
→ Deployment
→ Monitoring
→ Canonization

Required lifecycle:

Research
→ Evaluation
→ Candidate

→ Production

# Closing Statement

The future direction of the Phoenix Codex should emphasize fewer fundamental invariants,
stronger observability, stronger mutation governance, and clearer separation between
production engineering and research experimentation.
